Choose your route

Route 1 · Build it yourself

Make your own pivot tables and charts from the Raw Data sheet, with the Week 3 lab guide and the “In Google Sheets” lines in the brief.

Route 2 · Use the ready tables

Each dataset has extra sheets, “Q1 ready” to “Q9 ready”, with the tables and a chart for each question (questions 2, 4 and 7 have an “a” and a “b” sheet). Focus on reading the numbers and deciding.

Both routes are graded the same way: on your interpretation of the numbers and on the decisions you recommend. Choosing Route 2 costs you nothing.

Datasets and brief

The files are protected. Type the password from class; the files are unlocked in your browser.

The 9 questions (Part 1)

  1. Revenue vs quantity · are we truly expanding?
  2. Structural change and seasonality · what changed in 2025, and are low months a weakness?
  3. Volume vs value · more units, or smarter selling?
  4. Strategic and hidden categories · which three deserve investment, and which can we not lose?
  5. SKU pricing logic · popular, or simply expensive?
  6. Portfolio concentration · focused, or dangerously dependent?
  7. Discounts · do they help our economics, or only shift volume?
  8. Campaign impact · extra sales, or demand that was there anyway?
  9. Channel concentration · where is the value, are we overexposed?

What you hand in

  • Part 1, diagnosis: one answer for each of the 9 questions (one or two slides for Q2, Q4 and Q7).
  • Part 2, AI campaign: a campaign that answers ONE Part 1 finding. It must include a chain slide: your finding (question and number) → the campaign objective → one KPI you would track. Plus target audience, key message, visual direction and channels.
  • Appendix: extra tables and calculations, sources, the prompt log for every AI visual, and an AI-use disclosure slide.
  • Length: about 15–20 main slides. A busy executive must be able to follow it.
  • File: one PDF named Surname_Brand.pdf, uploaded to Final Project on Moodle by 18 November 2026.
  • Week 6: you present to the “brand board”. Speak in your own words, do not read; you will get one or two questions about your choices.

Example: one question, two answers

A made-up brand, Q7a (discount performance). The numbers are invented and do not come from any of the 8 datasets.

Weak answer

Title “Q7. Discount Performance”, a screenshot of the pivot table, and: “Discounted lines have an average quantity of 1.31 and full-price lines 1.01. Revenue per unit is 30 vs 40. Discounts weaken performance.”

The numbers are right, but nothing tells the board what they mean or what to do.

Strong answer

Headline
Discounts sell 30% more units, but we earn 25% less on each one.
Chart
Revenue per unit, full price vs discounted, bars from zero.
Key number
−25% revenue per unit (revenue / quantity).
So what
For a premium brand we are buying volume with margin, and 1 in 4 lines is now discounted.
Next step
Cap discounts at 20% and test a gift with purchase in Q4.
Source
Q7a ready.

Another student could reach a different conclusion from the same numbers, for example that discounts clear old stock without hurting a fast-fashion brand. That can also be a strong answer, if it is argued from the brand model.

Using AI: allowed, but show your thinking

  • Allowed: AI for ideas, wording, checking a formula, and for the Part 2 visuals.
  • Check every number: each number must come from your dataset, with its source under the chart.
  • Edit what AI writes: placeholders such as “[Chart: …]”, formula code, metrics we did not cover in class, or text addressed to “your brand” cost points under Professionalism.
  • Another reading: on at least one Part 1 slide, add a short note on an interpretation you considered and why you rejected it. This is where we see your own thinking.
  • Present, don't read: in Week 6 you speak in your own words and answer one or two questions about your choices.

Before you submit: checklist

  • Every answer slide: an insight headline with a number, one chart, the key number, a “so what”, a next step and a source line.
  • One axis per chart, bars from zero, units labelled. No pivot screenshots on the main slides.
  • At least one “another reading” note in Part 1.
  • Part 2 starts from one Part 1 finding (chain slide: finding → objective → KPI).
  • Appendix: prompt log for every AI visual, AI-use disclosure slide, sources.
  • No likeness of real people, no copied campaigns or logos; a real brand is labelled “student concept”.
